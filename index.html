About

How I think about building.

My path into AI wasn't a straight line. I started computer science because I enjoyed programming and problem-solving, but I didn't know what kind of problems I actually wanted to solve.

Two projects answered that question. The first was classifying patient conditions from 13,944 drug reviews. What absorbed me wasn't only the model; it was the engineering process: understanding messy text, representing language numerically, choosing honest evaluation metrics, and deploying a working system.

The second was undergraduate research on face anti-spoof detection, published at ICETCI-2025 in Springer Conference Proceedings. Working at publication depth taught me that every design decision needs a justification you are willing to put in writing.

Both experiences pointed to the same conclusion: I am interested in systems, not isolated models. I graduated with a B.Tech in Computer Science and Engineering (CGPA: 8.66). Today, I focus on building intelligent products that bridge research, engineering, and real-world impact while preparing for advanced study in Artificial Intelligence.

Mission

Technology should reduce cognitive load, not increase it.

Engineering Principles

How I Work

01
Understand the problem before choosing the tool.

Technology is a response to a problem, not the starting point.

02
The full system matters more than any single component.

Data, features, model, evaluation, deployment, and user value have to work together.

03
Choose the simplest approach that actually works.

Linear SVM worked for the NLP classifier because its assumptions matched the constraints.

04
Evaluation metrics are arguments, not scores.

Macro F1 mattered because class imbalance made accuracy alone misleading.

05
Research and engineering belong together.

The best work combines rigorous investigation with systems that can be built and used.

06
Measure what the system does for people.

A system is not ready until its value to real users is clear.
